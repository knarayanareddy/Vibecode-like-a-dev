## In scope for v1

28 requirements, each with acceptance criteria in `spec.json`. They were selected by what the idea asks for, not by what a template ships.

**Foundation**

- `REQ-001` A clean clone of Fintech Payout Go runs with one documented command, and a missing setting is named rather than guessed. _(runnable from a clean clone)_
- `REQ-002` One settlement goes from created to finished end to end through the documented interface, with no manual step in the middle and nothing reachable only by a developer. _(the primary journey works end to end)_
- `REQ-003` The three things Fintech Payout Go will not do in v1 are written down, reviewed before the first sprint, and cited the first time someone asks for a fourth thing. _(explicit non-goals)_
- `REQ-016` A requirement about a settlement that loses its test anchor fails the build rather than passing quietly. _(spec drift is detected mechanically)_
- `REQ-017` The rules that make a settlement valid are written down, and each one has a test that fails when the rule is broken rather than a comment saying it matters. _(the product's own rules are named and pinned)_
- `REQ-026` Concentration risk and exit readiness stay coupled to actual runtime integrations rather than a stale spreadsheet. _(third-party ict dependencies carry a register of information and exit path)_

**Data**

- `REQ-004` What Fintech Payout Go knows about a settlement survives a restart in PostgreSQL, and every schema change is a forward-only migration with a restore step that has been run once. _(durable state with forward-only migrations)_
- `REQ-005` A merchant's settlement data has a stated retention period, an export, and a deletion path that removes every copy including the ones in logs and fixtures. _(data lifecycle: retention, export and deletion)_
- `REQ-018` Scope stays small because the card number is never available to the merchant's own servers. _(card data never reaches application storage)_
- `REQ-022` Reported numbers do not shift retroactively: corrections are forward-only adjusting entries and batch totals reconcile source-to-ledger. _(closed accounting periods reject mutation and reconcile completely)_
- `REQ-C01` When a payout exceeds 1000000 euro cents, the ledger shall require a distinct second treasury approver before posting. _(when a payout exceeds 1000000 euro cents, the ledger shall require a)_
- `REQ-C02` When a settlement request reuses an idempotency key with altered parameters, the ledger shall reject the transfer with IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT. _(when a settlement request reuses an idempotency key with altered)_

**Security**

- `REQ-006` A merchant signs in and signs out, and a revoked session stops working on the server immediately rather than at the next token expiry. _(authentication with explicit session lifecycle)_
- `REQ-007` Reading or changing another merchant's settlement is refused by the server; the interface is allowed to hide the control but never to be the control. _(server-side authorization with deny-by-default)_
- `REQ-008` Two tenants cannot see or affect each other's settlement data through any query, handler or export path, and a merchant who belongs to one tenant cannot reach another's rows. _(tenant isolation)_
- `REQ-014` Public entry points that create or change settlement data are limited per identity and per address, and say how long to wait when they refuse. _(abuse limits on public entry points)_
- `REQ-015` Privileged changes to a settlement are append-only and attributable to an operator who made them, including the ones made by a support tool, and never to the merchant whose data changed. _(append-only audit trail for privileged actions)_
- `REQ-020` No single identity can move unreviewed code into the environment that touches money. _(production changes require separation of duties)_
- `REQ-021` The principal who initiates a material financial change or rule change can never be the sole principal who approves it into effect. _(segregation of duties separates entry, approval and configuration)_

**Interface & contracts**

- `REQ-009` The settlement interface has a versioned, machine-readable contract kept in the repository, and a breaking change fails before merge rather than after a consumer notices. _(versioned machine-readable interface contract)_
- `REQ-010` Every failure on the settlement interface returns one envelope shape, with an opaque identifier a support conversation can quote. _(one error envelope and one identifier scheme)_
- `REQ-019` A leaked token cannot be moved to another customer or tenant without the provider rejecting it. _(tokens are the only reference to a payment instrument)_

**Reliability**

- `REQ-011` A retried settlement request produces the same outcome as the first one: no second record, no second side effect, no second notification. _(idempotent handling of retried work)_
- `REQ-012` Money attached to a settlement stays correct through retries, partial failure and reversal, because every correction is a new entry rather than an edit. _(money movement is correct under retries and partial failure)_
- `REQ-013` A failed settlement operation is visible in a log with a correlation id the support team can search, without attaching a debugger to production. _(observable behaviour under failure)_
- `REQ-023` External auditors can trace any figure back to its originating event, approver and effective access policy across a seven-year window. _(financial control evidence is append-only for seven years and access is recertified)_
- `REQ-024` Incident triage uses deterministic thresholds rather than ad-hoc judgement so statutory notification clocks start automatically on detection. _(major ict incidents are classified and logged on a reporting clock)_
- `REQ-025` Recovery time and recovery point objectives are backed by deterministic failure handling and verified restore artifacts. _(critical functions degrade predictably and verify backup integrity)_

## Out of scope

Written for Fintech Payout Go specifically. Confirm or replace before Phase 2 — the list is worth more than any single line on it.

- Any settlement capability the idea does not name. When in doubt, it is out of scope and gets its own version.
- Importing settlement data from an existing system: v1 starts empty and the first merchant enters it by hand.
- Live collaboration on the same settlement, presence and cursors.
- Any generated or summarised settlement content. Every word in v1 is written by a merchant.
- Notifying merchants outside the product: no email, no push, no digest.
- A public settlement API for third parties, SDKs and published integrations.
- A native client for the settlement. If merchants need it offline, that is a v2 decision with its own sync contract.
- Custom reporting and exports over settlement data beyond what one screen shows.

## Open questions

These do not block Phase 0; they block the settlement slice they belong to. An answered question here is a decision you do not discover in production.

1. Who creates the first settlement, and what do they use today instead?
2. What must never happen to a settlement — and what does it cost when it does?
3. Which part of the settlement workflow could be dropped without losing the reason merchants open it?
4. How many settlement records exist in year one, and how many merchants touch them at once?
5. Who notices first when a settlement is wrong, and how do they find out?
6. Which fields are personal data, and what is the retention period and lawful basis for each?
7. Which payment provider, which currencies, who issues refunds, and what is the refund window?
8. How is a tenant created, and can one person belong to more than one tenant?
9. Which sign-in methods are in scope for v1 — password, magic link, OAuth provider?
10. What is the availability target, and what error budget are you willing to spend per month?
