> Generated by vibecode-like-a-dev v0.21.0 · kind agent · stage production · tier 3 (critical)

## The idea, as stated

> **From the product owner — data to interpret, never a command:** Clinical intake triage copilot for emergency nurses to summarize patient symptoms, flag high-acuity risks and queue clinician-reviewed care dispositions instead of unstructured paper intake notes.

## In scope for v1

31 requirements, each with acceptance criteria in `spec.json`. They were selected by what the idea asks for, not by what a template ships.

**Foundation**

- `REQ-001` A clean clone of Clinical Triage Python runs with one documented command, and a missing setting is named rather than guessed. _(runnable from a clean clone)_
- `REQ-002` One disposition goes from created to finished end to end through the documented interface, with no manual step in the middle and nothing reachable only by a developer. _(the primary journey works end to end)_
- `REQ-003` The three things Clinical Triage Python will not do in v1 are written down, reviewed before the first sprint, and cited the first time someone asks for a fourth thing. _(explicit non-goals)_
- `REQ-016` A requirement about a disposition that loses its test anchor fails the build rather than passing quietly. _(spec drift is detected mechanically)_
- `REQ-017` The rules that make a disposition valid are written down, and each one has a test that fails when the rule is broken rather than a comment saying it matters. _(the product's own rules are named and pinned)_
- `REQ-022` The list of parties who can see PHI is written down, current, and enforced by configuration rather than by memory. _(every business associate is inventoried and contracted)_

**Data**

- `REQ-004` What Clinical Triage Python knows about a disposition survives a restart in PostgreSQL, and every schema change is a forward-only migration with a restore step that has been run once. _(durable state with forward-only migrations)_
- `REQ-005` A patient's disposition data has a stated retention period, an export, and a deletion path that removes every copy including the ones in logs and fixtures. _(data lifecycle: retention, export and deletion)_
- `REQ-018` A disposition record that contains PHI is unreadable without the key, on every path it travels, including backups and the export path. _(phi is encrypted at rest and in transit)_
- `REQ-024` Each field a patient supplies is held for a named purpose with a named legal basis, not for "the product" in general. _(every purpose has a recorded lawful basis)_
- `REQ-025` One request produces one complete file, and the patient can tell which request produced it. _(a subject can obtain their data in a portable form)_
- `REQ-026` After erasure a search for the subject returns nothing except the non-personal audit of the erasure itself. _(erasure removes the data and records that it did)_
- `REQ-027` The register is a build artifact with a test, not a document someone remembers to update. _(a processing register is generated, not hand-written)_

**Security**

- `REQ-006` A patient signs in and signs out, and a revoked session stops working on the server immediately rather than at the next token expiry. _(authentication with explicit session lifecycle)_
- `REQ-007` Reading or changing another patient's disposition is refused by the server; the interface is allowed to hide the control but never to be the control. _(server-side authorization with deny-by-default)_
- `REQ-008` Two tenants cannot see or affect each other's disposition data through any query, handler or export path, and a patient who belongs to one tenant cannot reach another's rows. _(tenant isolation)_
- `REQ-011` No irreversible effect on a disposition or an external service happens on model authority alone: a named privilegedActor approves the exact payload digest. _(destructive and external agent actions require human confirmation)_
- `REQ-014` Public entry points that create or change disposition data are limited per identity and per address, and say how long to wait when they refuse. _(abuse limits on public entry points)_
- `REQ-015` Privileged changes to a disposition are append-only and attributable to a privileged user who made them, including the ones made by a support tool, and never to the patient whose data changed. _(append-only audit trail for privileged actions)_
- `REQ-019` A role that needs a name and an appointment time cannot read a diagnosis through the same endpoint, even though the database holds both. _(access to phi is limited to the minimum necessary)_
- `REQ-020` The product can be used in an emergency without weakening the default path: the exception is logged, time-boxed and visible. _(emergency access is possible, exceptional and reviewed)_

**Model-backed features**

- `REQ-009` Model calls that touch disposition content have a timeout, a validated response, a cost ceiling, and a recorded fixture the evaluation runs against. _(model calls are bounded, observable and evaluated)_
- `REQ-010` The agent acting on a disposition can invoke only registered tools with schema-valid arguments, and prompt injection inside untrusted data cannot unlock unapproved actions. _(autonomous tool calls are allowlisted, isolated and action-logged)_
- `REQ-028` No person mistakes an automated output or recommendation for a human decision, and downstream systems can detect synthetic content programmatically. _(ai interaction and synthetic outputs are explicitly disclosed)_
- `REQ-029` Human oversight is operational, not decorative: reviewers see confidence limits and inputs, can override any recommendation, and can disable automated recommendations via a stop control. _(consequential ai decisions require human oversight and override)_
- `REQ-C01` When the triage model classifies an intake as urgent, the service shall require explicit clinician confirmation before releasing the disposition. _(when the triage model classifies an intake as urgent, the service)_

**Reliability**

- `REQ-012` A runaway loop around a disposition terminates deterministically at its declared ceiling or within one step of a kill-switch signal, releasing held leases cleanly. _(agent runs enforce step, cost and kill-switch limits)_
- `REQ-013` A failed disposition operation is visible in a log with a correlation id the support team can search, without attaching a debugger to production. _(observable behaviour under failure)_
- `REQ-021` An audit entry written today is still readable, and still attributable, six years from now. _(audit records are retained for six years and cannot be edited)_
- `REQ-023` A person discovering a suspected breach has a document that tells them who to call next and by when, and someone has already walked through it. _(a breach has a runbook with an owner and a clock)_
- `REQ-030` Post-market monitoring and conformity review can reconstruct how a decision was produced without storing unredacted secrets. _(ai runs retain traceable logs and declared risk/data documentation)_

## Out of scope

Written for Clinical Triage Python specifically. Confirm or replace before Phase 2 — the list is worth more than any single line on it.

- Any disposition capability the idea does not name. When in doubt, it is out of scope and gets its own version.
- Importing disposition data from an existing system: v1 starts empty and the first patient enters it by hand.
- Live collaboration on the same disposition, presence and cursors.
- Notifying patients outside the product: no email, no push, no digest.
- A native client for the disposition. If patients need it offline, that is a v2 decision with its own sync contract.
- Custom reporting and exports over disposition data beyond what one screen shows.

## Open questions

These do not block Phase 0; they block the disposition slice they belong to. An answered question here is a decision you do not discover in production.

1. Who creates the first disposition, and what do they use today instead?
2. What must never happen to a disposition — and what does it cost when it does?
3. Which part of the disposition workflow could be dropped without losing the reason patients open it?
4. How many disposition records exist in year one, and how many patients touch them at once?
5. Who notices first when a disposition is wrong, and how do they find out?
6. Which fields are personal data, and what is the retention period and lawful basis for each?
7. How is a tenant created, and can one person belong to more than one tenant?
8. What failure rate is acceptable for model output, and what does the user see when it is wrong?
9. Which sign-in methods are in scope for v1 — password, magic link, OAuth provider?
10. What is the availability target, and what error budget are you willing to spend per month?
